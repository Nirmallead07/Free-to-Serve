# Free to Serve

A private reflection on authority and community life for women and men religious and clergy.

Prepared by Fr. Solomon Anthony Nirmalan, CMF.

## About the tool

The reflection has three parts.

**Part One: What would most likely happen?** Ten situations from community and diocesan life. The person chooses what would most likely happen in their own community, not what should happen. "This does not apply to me" is left out of the score.

**Part Two: How often is this true?** Thirty-three statements about the present community or diocese, answered never, sometimes, often or always. Some are reverse-scored. Two further statements check gently for answers shaped by loyalty rather than experience.

**Part Three: A closing check.** Five yes or no questions. A yes to any of the first four shows the strong-signs message whatever the scores. A yes to the fifth shows a support message, with the 1926 National Mental Health Helpline, before any other result.

The results cover ten areas: freedom to speak, how concerns are received, use of spiritual authority, connection and outside support, respect and dignity, belonging and approval, personal freedom and care, freedom to discern your path, how this affects me, and honest and fair decisions. Each area is shown as healthy signs (under 34%), some warning signs (34 to 59%) or strong warning signs (60% and above). These cut-offs are provisional until a pilot.

The person can save the results as a PDF called "Reflection Notes", with an optional password. The PDF is made on the phone itself.

The tool is a reflection, not a validated measurement instrument or a diagnosis. Results should not be used to evaluate any person or community.

The whole tool is one file, `index.html`, with the PDF library (jsPDF 2.5.2, MIT licence) built in. Answers are kept only in the page's memory while it is open. Nothing is sent anywhere and nothing is stored on the phone.
